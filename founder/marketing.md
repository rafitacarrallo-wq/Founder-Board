# Marketing — Demo-to-Deal (working name)

Market is global / anglophone, so this plan is in **English** (the package's language
rule). Every message answers an objection or reason the buyer panel actually raised
(`panel/results.md`). Panel quotes are research, never used as testimonials.

## The one thing the evidence forces

The 100-buyer panel is blunt: **cold outreach converts ~0%.** "Busca referencias"
and "Hazlo-tú-mismo" buyers bought at 0%; the only behaviour that bought was
**"board-pressured" (67%)**. And the top "what would flip me" was, over and over,
*"a founder I respect or my VC vouching for it."*

So marketing is **not** a cold-outbound machine. It is a **referral + VC-partnership
engine** that reaches board-pressured founders through the very people applying the
pressure (their investors), backed by proof (the founding-cohort cases). Cold
LinkedIn/email at scale is explicitly **not** the strategy — the data says it wastes
money here.

## Step 2: positioning

```
For seed / Series A founders in AI, logistics and supply chain whose enterprise
deals stall in the demo, Demo-to-Deal is the specialist that rebuilds the demo into
a 7-minute value pitch that advances deals — unlike pitch coaches or fractional
sales leaders, it is one fixed-scope 3-week sprint, run by an operations insider,
measured on your advance-to-second-meeting rate.
```

Three versions (recommend #1):
1. **"The demo-to-deal specialist for deep-tech founders."** (recommended — owns the
   narrow category the board asked us to own; names the exact moment.)
2. "Stop losing enterprise deals in the demo." (pain-first; good for ads.)
3. "Your demo speaks engineering. Your buyer speaks ROI. We translate." (mechanism.)

Evidence: the panel universally validated the pain ("this is exactly where I get
stuck"); the competitor map (`competitors.md`) shows the "enterprise-sales demo ×
niche" quadrant is empty, and that pitch coaches/fractionals are the nearest
alternatives to differentiate from.

## Step 3: channels

| channel | why it fits this buyer | rough cost | how we know it worked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **VC / accelerator partnerships** (primary) | reaches board-pressured founders via their investors; referral is the #1 flip | time + rev-share/referral fee | warm intros per month; % of portfolio booking an audit |
| **Founder referrals** (primary) | 0% buy cold vs. high on peer vouch; every delivered sprint should spawn an intro | built into delivery (ask at the case step) | referrals per delivered sprint (target ≥1) |
| **Teardown content** (secondary) | demonstrates insider criterion before they pay (kills "an outsider doesn't get my product"); builds the category | ~4h/week of own time | audit requests from content; saves/shares |
| **A thin warm-outbound layer** (tertiary) | targeted, *personalised* notes to founders who just raised (board pressure is fresh), referencing a specific friction in their public demo | own time | reply rate; not a volume play |

What we are **not** doing and why: broad cold email/LinkedIn sequences, paid social,
SEO. The panel shows cold doesn't convert for this buyer, and the business is
capacity-bound (max ~2 sprints/month) — we need a handful of high-intent, warm
leads, not volume. Spend goes to partnerships and proof, not ad clicks.

## Step 4: the 30-day campaign (launch = day 0)

Goal of the launch: fill the **founding cohort (5 slots)** with board-pressured
founders via warm channels, and publish the first teardown to seed the category.

| day | channel | what goes out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -14 | Partnerships | Email 8-12 target VCs/accelerators (AI/logistics/supply-chain thesis): "I fix the demo-to-deal gap for your portfolio; 5 founding slots." |
| -12 | Content | Publish Teardown #1: an anonymised "why this demo stalls" breakdown (hook 1). |
| -10 | Founder network | DM 15-20 founders you already know for intros, not sales (hook 7). |
| -7 | Content | Teardown #2 + the advance-rate metric explainer (hook 3). |
| -5 | Partnerships | Follow up VCs; offer a free 20-min teardown for one portfolio co each. |
| -3 | Content | "Founding cohort: 5 slots" announcement with the guarantee (hook 2, 9). |
| 0 | All | Launch: open the 5 founding slots + the €2,900 Demo Audit entry (hook 10). |
| +2 | Content | Teardown #3 on an AI/ops demo pattern (hook 5). |
| +5 | Warm outbound | Personalised notes to founders who raised in the last 90 days (hook 6). |
| +7 | Review | Day-7 check: slots booked? audits booked? intros in? |
| +9 | Content | Share the method: "the 7-minute value-pitch structure" (hook 4). |
| +12 | Partnerships | Second VC wave + any early audit result (no fabricated numbers). |
| +14 | Review | Day-14 check against the success line. |
| +16 | Founder network | Ask first audit clients for one intro each (referral loop on). |

**When there's nothing new to say:** post a teardown or a short "insider note" on what
actually moves an operations buyer. The teardown stream IS the content engine.

### Ten hooks (each answers a panel objection/reason)

1. "Your demo is technically brilliant. That's exactly why it's losing the deal." (reframes DIY/pride)
2. "We tie our fee to your advance-to-second-meeting rate. If it doesn't move, we re-run it free." (kills "no guarantee")
3. "Enterprise deals take months — but the demo's advance rate changes in week one. That's what we move." (kills "3 weeks won't change my pipeline")
4. "Last slide first: show the ROI before the architecture." (the method; kills feature-dump)
5. "Your buyer runs operations, not a code review. Here's what they actually score." (insider criterion)
6. "You just raised. Your board wants enterprise traction. The demo is where it leaks." (speaks to the only segment that buys — board-pressured)
7. "Not selling — ask your VC if they know a founder whose demos stall. That's who I help." (referral-first)
8. "Not a fractional VP, not a pitch coach: one sprint, one metric, one operator who's sat on the buyer's side." (differentiates from alternatives)
9. "Founding cohort: 5 slots, documented before/after, founder price. Then it's €9,500." (real urgency + proof build)
10. "Not ready for the sprint? Start with a €2,900 teardown of your current demo — credited if you go on." (low-risk entry)

## Step 5: budget and the numbers

**CAC ceiling (from the CFO).** Contribution per sprint ≈ **€8,487**. On a single sprint
you could theoretically pay up to ~€8,487 to win a client and still break even on that
deal — but for a healthy business keep CAC **well under 25-30% of contribution, i.e.
target ≤ ~€2,000-2,500 per client**, with referral/partnership CAC far lower. Because
the panel buys on referral, the cheapest and best-converting acquisition is warm — so
the target blended CAC should be low by design.

**Budget split (lean, ~€1,000-1,500/month to start):**
- Partnerships & referral rev-share: ~50% (the channel that actually converts).
- Content production (editing, light design for teardowns): ~30%.
- Tools already in fixed costs (Sales Navigator, scheduling): the rest.
- Paid ads: **€0 at launch** — revisit only if a warm channel proves a repeatable, sub-ceiling CAC.

**Three numbers to watch weekly (and the "change something" line):**
1. **Warm intros / qualified calls per week** — if < 2 after week 2, the partnership
   motion isn't working; change the VC pitch or the target list.
2. **Audit→sprint conversion** — if < ~30%, the audit isn't demonstrating enough value;
   fix the audit deliverable.
3. **Referrals per delivered sprint** — if < 1, the delivery isn't producing advocates;
   add the explicit referral ask at the case step.

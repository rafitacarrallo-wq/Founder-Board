### Shortlist (3) and the checks to run yourself

**A. Demo-to-Deal** · **B. Narrata** · **C. [FounderName] Studio** (e.g. "Carrallo Studio")

This trio hedges: A = descriptive/SEO-clear but hard to protect; B = ownable/
trademarkable invented mark; C = the credibility of a named insider, which the panel
rewards (they buy the *operator*, not a faceless brand).

- **Trademark** (class 35 advertising/business + class 41 training; confirm classes):
  - EUIPO (EU): https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/ (TMview) and https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/
  - USPTO (US): https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/
  - UKIPO (UK): https://www.gov.uk/search-for-trademark
  - WIPO global: https://branddb.wipo.int/
- **Domain:** check .com first, then .io / .co, at your registrar (don't assume).
  "Demo-to-Deal" .com is likely taken/long → consider demotodeal.com, getnarrata.com,
  trynarrata.com, or the founder-name domain. Verify, don't assume.
- **Handles:** LinkedIn (primary for this B2B buyer), X, YouTube (for teardowns),
  Instagram (low priority). Check each directly.
- **Confusion:** keep clear of the competitors in `competitors.md` (Great Demo!,
  C2B, Trebal & Chowsky, Andy Raskin). "Demos That Close"/"Great Demo!" are close —
  avoid.

My lean: if you want to build a protectable asset and a category, **Narrata** (or
another invented mark) + a descriptive tagline. If you want to lead with operator
credibility now and build the category later, **[FounderName] Studio**. "Demo-to-Deal"
is the clearest *descriptor* — keep it as the tagline/category even if the company
name is B or C.

## Step 3: voice and promise

- **One-line promise:** *"Your technical demo, rebuilt into the 7 minutes that move an
  enterprise buyer from 'interesting' to 'next meeting'."*
- **Taglines (3):**
  1. "From demo to deal." (recommended — the category, doubles as name A)
  2. "Make your buyer say yes in seven minutes."
  3. "You built the product. We make it sell itself."
- **Voice — three adjectives:**
  - **Operator, not guru.** Do: speak from having sat on the buyer's side ("here's what
    an ops buyer actually scores"). Don't: motivational-speaker hype, "crush your
    quota".
  - **Specific & measurable.** Do: name the metric (advance-to-second-meeting), use
    concrete teardown detail. Don't: vague "storytelling magic".
  - **Blunt & respectful.** Do: tell a technical founder the hard truth about their demo
    plainly. Don't: condescend about engineers "not getting sales".
  - **Before/after rewrite** of a line from `pitch.md`:
    - Before (feature-ish): "A rebuilt 7-minute value narrative — ROI, risk and
      operational efficiency, in the buyer's language."
    - After (voice): "Seven minutes. Your buyer hears cost, risk and headcount saved —
      in that order — before they ever see the architecture."

## Step 4: the look (brief)

- **Colour** (hex + job + contrast):
  - **Ink `#0E1A2B`** (near-black navy) — primary text/surfaces; authority, deep-tech.
    White text on Ink ≈ 15:1 → passes WCAG AA/AAA.
  - **Signal `#2FB37A`** (confident green) — the "advance/forward" accent (deals moving
    up); CTAs. On Ink, white text on a Signal button passes AA for large text; for body
    use Ink text on white, not on Signal. Verify each real pairing before shipping.
  - **Paper `#F6F7F5`** (warm off-white) — background; Ink on Paper ≈ 14:1 → AA/AAA.
  - Use green sparingly (accent only), so it reads as "progress", not decoration.
- **Type** (free / licensed, with source):
  - Display: **Space Grotesk** (SIL OFL, free) — technical-but-human, good for a
    deep-tech audience. https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Space+Grotesk
  - Text: **Inter** (SIL OFL, free) — neutral, legible on screen.
    https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter
  - Only fonts you have a licence for; both above are OFL/free for commercial use.
- **Logo brief:** must say "forward motion / a step change", work on a LinkedIn avatar
  at 32px, a slide footer, and a plain email signature. Avoid: speech bubbles, generic
  "rocket/graph-up" clichés, anything resembling a pitch-deck template logo. A simple
  wordmark in Space Grotesk with one forward-motion mark (e.g. a ">" / arrow turning
  "demo" into "deal") is enough for a solo boutique. If you generate concepts with an
  image tool, mark them **concepts**, not a finished logo.
- **First five touchpoints** (the product the buyer sees):
  1. **The one-page site / LinkedIn headline:** the promise + "founding cohort, 5 slots"
     + the audit entry.
  2. **The teardown post template:** consistent title card ("Why this demo stalls #N"),
     Ink/Signal, Space Grotesk.
  3. **The proposal / audit deliverable:** Paper background, Ink text, Signal accents,
     the agreed metric on page 1.
  4. **The booking confirmation:** plain, warm, sets the week-1 expectation and the
     metric baseline ask.
  5. **The reply to a tough question / first complaint:** blunt-and-respectful voice;
     restate the guarantee.
